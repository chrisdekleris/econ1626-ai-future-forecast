The range of estimates

How much will artificial intelligence add to Australia's productivity growth? The studies reviewed by the Productivity Commission (PC) range from 0.05 to 1.3 percentage points a year (Productivity Commission 2025). The Commission's own central case is 0.23 points (Productivity Commission 2025). CommBank's chief economist puts economies that adopt AI "deeply and widely" at 0.8 to 1.0 points (Yeaman 2026). At the bottom, Acemoglu's (2025) ceiling of 0.66 per cent over a decade works out to under 0.07 points a year.

The highest estimate is more than twenty times the lowest. Most of that gap probably comes down to diffusion, meaning how quickly ordinary firms change the way they work to use AI.

Australia's productivity gain from AI by 2030 will depend mostly on how quickly its small firms adopt it. Owning the models or the data centres probably matters less than current policy assumes. Building AI locally is worthwhile when it removes a real barrier to adoption, and Maincode, a Melbourne AI developer, appears to be moving in that direction.

The forecast runs to 2030 because that is the year Jobs and Skills Australia's (2025) fastest adoption pathway reaches 50 per cent. That is long enough for adoption to show up in productivity data, and soon enough to check the forecast against what happens.
